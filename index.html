The problem

A structural gap — not a shortage of capital

Europe’s funding map has a hole in the middle, and each existing channel stops short of it. Economics, scale, liquidity and standards — the market fails four ways at once.

BELOW THE GAP

The bank balance sheet

Banks still lend comfortably below ~€20m. As the ticket grows, return on regulatory capital thins — so banks ration, they don’t refuse, and the borrower is no worse. The channel that used to serve this size is retreating from it.

ABOVE THE GAP

The public bond market

Benchmark economics — rating, prospectus, syndicate — start near €250m. Below that the market barely looks, and no syndicate desk clears its cost base much under €75–100m.

WHAT REMAINS

Slow, bilateral, locked

House-bank loans and private credit are bilateral and locked — no exit, no market. The Schuldschein standardised the loan, not a tradeable security: no ISIN, no trustee, transfer only by consent, and its bank pool takes internally rated, investment-grade-to-crossover names only. Leasing solves the machine, not the company. Investors reach the segment only through funds whose fees eat 1.5–2.5% of the return.

Two different thresholds, often confused: syndicate desks stop clearing their cost base around €75–100m (a fee-economics limit), while benchmark public-bond economics start near €250m (an access limit). Our own dataset of 60 verified non-Nordic issuers shows the Nordic format effectively does not exist below €25m and averages €100–110m market-wide. Design headroom to €300m; growth via repeat issuers (Katjes grew from ~€110m to €185m outstanding).
The missing middle: €20–100m of debt for proven mid-caps. The root cause is arranger cost structures that make deals this size unprofitable — a missing-infrastructure problem, not a capital-shortage problem. Standardisation and automation are what build the rail.
Why now

Five forces converge on this gap

Europe’s own competitiveness agenda — from the Draghi report to the Savings and Investments Union — names exactly this problem: the continent doesn’t lack capital, it lacks an efficient market that turns savings into investment. Underneath the policy, five things changed at once.

€800bnper year in additional investment the Draghi report says Europe needs. A political mandate proves urgency — never a solution. That part stays ours to prove.
~€300bnof European savings leaves Europe every year for want of efficient markets at home. The capital is real; the rail is missing.
Our positionWe don’t wait for the Capital Markets Union. We build a working mechanism inside today’s framework — for the companies the gap hits now.

Regulation pushes banks out

Basel capital rules make mid-cap credit expensive to hold, so banks retreat from exactly this size. Investment-grade benchmark fees run 0.25–0.45%; mid-cap placements historically cost 2.5–5% all-in. Below ≈€75–100m no syndicate desk clears its cost base — healthy mid-caps stay trapped in loan formats they have outgrown.

Standardisation targets 1.0–1.5% — then the coupon.

Private credit and Schuldschein at their limit

Private credit is saturated, single-lender and illiquid. Varta (≈90% haircut via StaRUG cram-down), BayWa (€800m+ across ~255 holders) and KTM (70% write-down) broke Schuldschein’s implicit-safety promise. The market didn’t shrink — ~€24bn in 2025 — but investors turned selective, pushing unrated, smaller and non-German names towards alternatives.

We standardise explicitly priced, governed risk — never “safer”.

The Nordic precedent works — just not here

Standardised, trustee-governed, tradeable bonds run at scale in the Nordics, arranged by houses like Pareto, and non-Nordic issuers are already a large share of that primary volume. Frankfurt manages >€700m in dedicated Nordic HY money; family offices doubled private-debt allocations to 4–5%; green mandates are starved of real EUR mid-cap paper.

The money is already in the format — just not in this market.

AI collapses the preparation cost

The work that made a €30m deal uneconomic was never the investing — it was the preparation: reading the registers, reconciling the accounts, assembling and checking the dossier. That is now machine work with human sign-off, and it takes issuer preparation from weeks to hours. It is the enabler, not the innovation.

A standard, once, for everyone

Nordic bond terms are documented, tested and adopted — not invented here. One covenant menu, one disclosure set, one process. Counsel reviews the delta rather than two hundred pages from scratch, and every deal after the first is cheaper than the one before it.

None of these existed together five years ago. That is the whole timing argument.
The platform

We don’t automate investing.
We automate everything before investing.

Platforms that tried to replace the banks failed — not on technology, but on missing investor networks and credibility. We do the opposite: an asset-light layer that standardises and prepares, anchored to a licensed partner who does the regulated work. Automation applies to preparation, never to the decision; licensed intermediaries stay central; and the fixed cost per deal drops below the €20–100m viability threshold. That is the whole mechanism.

Regulatory architecture

Regulation is part of the product design

VanillaBonds is deliberately built to own the software layer, not the regulatory layer. Exemption by design, not evasion: we sit inside the professional-only, €100,000-denomination private-placement lanes the law already leaves open, and the regulated acts sit with a licensed firm.

MiFID II / WpIG

Investor onboarding, suitability and placement run under a licensed investment firm’s licence, with VanillaBonds acting as its tied agent (vertraglich gebundener Vermittler) under a Haftungsdach. We hold no client money and take no principal risk.

CRA Regulation

The dossier delivers sourced facts and comparability — never a rating, a score or a ranking of creditworthiness within the meaning of Regulation (EC) 1060/2009. The credit judgment stays with the investor, by design.

Prospectus Regulation

Qualified investors only, €100,000 minimum denominations, in reliance on the applicable private-placement exemption. The full placement and admission structure is confirmed with securities counsel and the licensed host before the first transaction.

Trading venue

Existing regulated rails first — Börse Frankfurt Open Market or Euronext Oslo — rather than building a venue. Own infrastructure only where volume ever justifies it.

The intended architecture keeps VanillaBonds an infrastructure and information layer rather than a fully licensed investment firm. That perimeter is under formal review with securities counsel and prospective licensed hosts; no regulated activity is undertaken before it is settled.
The mechanism

The issuer fact sheet

The dossier is the product. An AI extraction engine reads the issuer’s primary documents and assembles a standardised fact sheet — the investor-facing front page of the deal. AI is the enabler, not the innovation: what it changes is the cost of preparing a €30m credit to institutional standard.

01 — EXTRACT

Primary sources, not narrative

Trade registers, annual accounts, confirmed collateral, loan and lease schedules. The engine reads them and populates a fixed schema, identical across every issuer — which is what makes two deals comparable at all. Issuer preparation compresses from weeks to hours.

02 — VERIFY

Every figure carries its source

Each field resolves to a document, a page and a status, and every number is approved by a human before it enters the sheet. Computed metrics use approved figures only; anything unapproved is visibly pending rather than quietly included. The dossier is auditable line by line.

The format

One standard. Six to eight weeks. Transferable from day one.

  • Senior secured, trustee-governed — Nordic Trustee model: if the platform disappeared tomorrow, a tradeable security with a trustee remains.
  • Floating-rate note, 3m EURIBOR + 450–975 — 3–4 year tenor, standard call schedule.
  • Covenants from a standard menu — incurrence-based net leverage, FCCR, dividend basket, change-of-control put; co-written with the founding investor circle we are assembling now.
  • Standardised disclosure — one data room, quarterly reporting through the platform, covenant certificates on file.
  • Listed & transferable — Börse Frankfurt Open Market / Euronext Oslo; institutional denominations, €100,000 minimum.
  • 6–8 weeks from mandate to settlement — one process, one document set, a standing professional book.
Transferability is structural — trustee-administered, CSD-settled, admitted to trading — and it is the exit private credit does not have. Depth is a different question: mid-cap secondary is thin today, and we say so rather than promise a two-way market we have not yet built.

The credit filter

What we decline is the product

Visible minimum criteria

Deal Profile #1 is published, hard and boring: Germany first — Bavarian and German industrial and energy-transition mid-caps · €20–100m band, first transactions targeted at €20–40m · EBITDA ≥ €8–10m · net leverage ≤ 3.5× · senior secured · 3–4 years · refinancing, capex or acquisition trigger · ongoing disclosure.

Company-scale, not asset-scale

We finance bundled programmes, intangible-heavy investment and acquisition-driven capex — the needs asset finance cannot reach. A single machine leases fine; that deal isn’t ours. This is the line that separates a bond from a lease, and we hold it.

Visible refusal, published statistics

Everything outside the profile is declined — visibly, even when it costs revenue — and declined issuers are, with consent, referred to private-credit partners. Every quarter we publish anonymised filter data. One avoidable default in the first ten deals is deadlier than ten missed deals.

This segment produces real defaults — our own 60-name dataset contains one default and one standstill. The book prices the risk; the standard governs it. We make both visible rather than pretend the risk away.
Landscape

Reference points, not rivals

Every predecessor solved one piece of the puzzle. None solved all four at once — standardisation, automation, distribution and tradability.

The Nordic bond marketProves that standardised, trustee-governed, tradeable bonds work at scale for exactly this size of company — just not in continental Europe.
vc tradeProves issuance digitises at scale — >€14bn placed — and that a bank-backed venue wins distribution: BayernLB, Helaba and RBI hold 10% each. What it distributes is a loan, so transferability is the one thing it cannot add.
SchuldscheinProves the demand it cannot carry: ~€24bn in 2025, but a loan, not a security — no ISIN, no trustee, no collective action clauses, transfer only by consent. Its bank buyers hold it precisely because a loan avoids mark-to-market. A different pool, by construction.
Mittelstandsanleihen / KMU-AnleihenProve small-ticket issuer demand is real — 33 issues and €1.1bn placed in 2024 alone (IR.on) — and that the format fails without a credit filter: the 2010–16 wave collapsed in defaults. Hence our published deal profile and visible refusal.
Firstwire · Nivaura · Synd-XProve that technology alone is insufficient. Each tried to disintermediate; none assembled the investor network. This is precisely why we are partner-led.
Bank loans · private credit · leasingThe alternatives a CFO actually weighs: retreating above €20m, illiquid and single-lender, and per machine rather than per company.
Our claim is not that nobody tried. It is that combining standardisation, automation and incumbent partnership changes the economics — so we complement the market rather than try to replace it.
